## 1. Catch

So the match is over. And your head is still on the court. It's replaying the easy ball you should have put away. The volley into the net. The second serve that didn't come. Maybe something your partner said, or the silence when they didn't say anything. Round and round, like a highlight reel of only the worst parts. And every time it plays, it gets a little louder.

You don't have to stop it. You don't have to do anything right now, actually. Just stay where you are, in the car, on a bench, at home, and let me talk for a few minutes. You can keep driving, keep packing your bag, whatever you're doing. Just let this voice be a bit louder than the one in your head.

---

## 2. What the replay does

Here's something Neville would point out right away. The match itself is finished. It happened once, and it's done. The ball isn't in the net anymore. The point is over. What's still going on is the replay. And the replay is not happening on the court. It's happening in your imagination.

That matters. Because for Neville, imagination isn't just daydreaming. It's the thing that creates. Whatever you go over again and again, whatever you keep feeling as true, that's what you're assuming. And what you assume hardens into fact. So every time you replay that volley into the net and tell yourself, there it is again, I always choke when it counts, you're not remembering the game. You're rehearsing a state. You're practising being the person who chokes. And you're doing it with feeling, which is exactly how assumptions get made.

The game didn't do that. The replay is doing it. And the replay is yours. Which means you can change it.

---

## 3. Revision

This is what Neville called revision. You take something that happened today, and before you go to sleep, you rewrite it the way you would have wanted it. Not to lie to yourself about what happened. The score is the score. You revise it because you don't want to carry that version into tomorrow. You'd rather carry the one you choose.

Let's take the one that stings most. For most of us it's a ball like this. An easy one. You hear it come off their racket, soft, a bit too high, the kind you've put away a hundred times. You've got all the time in the world, and somewhere in that time your head starts talking. Don't miss this one. Your grip gets tight, your arm goes stiff, and then there's that dull sound of the ball in the net. Your partner says nothing. You walk back to your spot, and the whole court goes very quiet. That's the one your head keeps going back to. Whatever your version of it is, that's the one.

Now we go through it again. And this time I'll tell it the way it goes, and you just follow the feeling of it. Same court. Same partner next to you. You hear the ball come off their racket, soft and high, and your body already knows where it's going. Your feet are moving before you've even thought about it. Two small steps, and you feel your weight settle, even on both feet. Your shoulders are loose. Your grip is soft. There's no voice in your head this time, just the sound of your own breath going out. And then that clean, hard pop off the middle of your racket. You feel it right up your arm. Low into the gap, and you hear it bounce twice before either of them gets close.

Your partner shouts, Vamos. You turn around, and you feel that little knock of rackets touching. You walk back to your spot, and your feet are light on the court. You hear your partner bounce the next ball, ready to serve, and you're already waiting for it. Nothing to make up for. That point was just a point, and it went the way points go when you're simply playing.

Let's go through it once more, a little slower. The soft sound off their racket. Your feet already moving. Two small steps, weight settled. Breath going out. The clean pop, right up your arm. Two bounces. Vamos. The knock of rackets. The walk back, light. That's how that point went.

That's the point you keep. And notice, you didn't need to see anything for that. Neville said feeling is the secret. Not the picture, the feeling of it being done. The pop off the racket, the knock of rackets, the shout. Do it until it feels natural. Until the new version is the one that comes up first. Every time the old one comes back tonight, just go through the new one over it. The pop, the bounces, Vamos. That's all revision is.

## 5. Something that went right

Your head only kept the misses. But there was more in that match. A shot that landed exactly where you wanted. A long rally where you were completely in it. A laugh between points. Something small is enough. Maybe it was a rally that went on and on, ten, twelve shots, that thud of the ball off the glass and you getting it back every time. Your legs burning a little, your breath loud, your whole body in it, and for those few seconds there was no score and no head talking, just the next shot. And then it ended, maybe with your shot, maybe with theirs, and someone laughed because it was such a good rally. That kind of moment. You had at least one of those today, even if your head didn't keep it.

That happened too. Today. That's just as true as the volley into the net. And it's the one worth replaying.

---

## 6. Done

Here's the last thing. This match is over. Not just the score, the whole thing. It's in the past now, and the past doesn't need you anymore. You played. You showed up, you moved, you went for your shots. That part is done, and it was yours.

The next match starts empty. Whenever that is, tomorrow, next week, it doesn't know anything about today. You walk onto that court fresh, and you get to try again. Not to prove anything. Just for the game itself.

So let this one go. You can let it be done now. Go on with your day.
