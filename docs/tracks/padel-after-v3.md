## 1. Catch

So the match is over. And your head is still on the court. It's replaying the easy ball you should have put away. The volley into the net. The second serve that didn't come. Maybe a look from your partner, or one you think you saw. Round and round, like a highlight reel of only the worst parts. And every time it plays, it gets a little louder.

You don't have to stop it. You don't have to do anything right now, actually. Just stay where you are, in the car, on a bench, at home, and let me talk for a few minutes. You can keep driving, keep packing your bag, whatever you're doing. Just let this voice be a bit louder than the one in your head.

---

## 2. What the replay does

Here's something Neville would point out right away. The match itself is finished. It happened once, and it's done. The ball isn't in the net anymore. The point is over. What's still going on is the replay. And the replay is not happening on the court. It's happening in your imagination.

That matters. Because for Neville, imagination isn't just daydreaming. It's the thing that creates. Whatever you go over again and again, whatever you keep feeling as true, that's what you're assuming. And what you assume hardens into fact. So every time you replay that volley into the net and tell yourself, there it is again, I always choke when it counts, you're not remembering the game. You're rehearsing a state. You're practising being the person who chokes. And you're doing it with feeling, which is exactly how assumptions get made.

The game didn't do that. The replay is doing it. And the replay is yours. Which means you can change it.

---

## 3. Revision

This is what Neville called revision. You take something that happened today, and before you go to sleep, you rewrite it the way you would have wanted it. Not to lie to yourself about what happened. The score is the score. You revise it because you don't want to carry that version into tomorrow. You'd rather carry the one you choose.

Let's take the one that stings most. For most of us it's a ball like this. An easy one. It comes high and slow to the net, the kind you've put away a hundred times. You're standing there, you've got all the time in the world, and somewhere in that time your head starts talking. Don't miss this one. And your arm goes stiff, the racket comes through a little late, and the ball goes into the net. Your partner says nothing. You walk back to your spot and the whole court feels very quiet. That's the clip your head keeps playing. Whatever your version of it is, that's the one.

Now we play it again. And this time I'll tell it the way it goes. Same court, same light, same partner next to you. The ball comes high and slow to the net. And you see it early, right off the other racket. Your feet are already moving before you've even thought about it. You're there in two small steps, balanced, loose in the shoulders. There's no voice in your head this time, just the ball getting bigger. Your racket comes up early and easy. And you put it away. Clean. Low into the gap between them, and it bounces twice before either of them gets close.

Your partner lets out a short shout. You turn around, and there's that little tap of rackets. You walk back to your spot, and your feet feel light on the court. Your partner bounces the next ball, ready to serve, and you're already waiting for it. Nothing to make up for. That point was just a point, and it went the way points go when you're simply playing.

Let's go through it once more, a little slower. The ball high and slow. You see it early. Two small steps. Racket up, easy. Into the gap, two bounces. The shout, the tap of rackets, the walk back. That's how that point went. Same ball, clean hit, into the gap.

That's the point you keep. Neville would say: do it until it feels natural. Until the new version is the one that comes up first. It doesn't have to be perfect tonight. Every time the old clip shows up again, just play the new one over it. That's all revision is.

[pause 6s]

I choose myself and my peace.

## 5. Something that went right

Your head only kept the misses. But there was more in that match. A shot that landed exactly where you wanted. A long rally where you were completely in it. A laugh between points. Something small is enough. Maybe it was a rally that went on and on, ten, twelve shots, the ball coming off the glass and you getting it back every time. Your legs were working, your eyes were on the ball and nowhere else, and for those few seconds there was no score and no head talking, just the next shot. And then it ended, maybe with your shot, maybe with theirs, and someone laughed because it was such a good rally. That kind of moment. You had at least one of those today, even if your head didn't keep it.

That happened too. Today. That's just as true as the volley into the net. And it's the one worth replaying.

---

## 6. Done

Here's the last thing. This match is over. Not just the score, the whole thing. It's in the past now, and the past doesn't need you anymore. You played. You showed up, you moved, you went for your shots. That part is done, and it was yours.

The next match starts empty. Whenever that is, tomorrow, next week, it doesn't know anything about today. You walk onto that court fresh, and you get to try again. Not to prove anything. Just for the game itself.

So let this one go. You can let it be done now. Go on with your day.
