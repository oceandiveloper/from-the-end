# Midday Reset v2 "Come down": v3 test version with audio tags

Status: **test, 2026-11-11.** Same text as `midday-reset-v2.md`, prepared for ElevenLabs v3: audio tags (`[softly]`, `[warmly]` …) and "…" for short thinking pauses. Paragraphs without a pause between them are merged into one segment, because v3 takes no previous/next text and the tone could jump at every segment border.
Voiced twice for comparison: "FTE Mentor" (Voice Design c3, `XOOf9U64tEfzTJ4rENa8`) and "FTE Coach" (Voice Design b3, `fxFGU8s6GbMmPPM9dxRl`), model `eleven_v3`, stability 0.5, style 0, speed 0.95.
Kevin's reason (2026-11-11): the library voices and James have no emotional weight; v3 with tags was the only setting that had some, and it isn't rushed.

---

## 1. Step away

[softly] Okay. You've stopped. [warmly] That's the hardest part, and it's done. You've been at the screen for hours, jumping between things, holding a lot at once. And now your head is still running at that speed… even though your hands have stopped. That buzz behind your forehead. The tightness around your eyes. The feeling that something is still open somewhere. [gently] That's not you being stressed about anything in particular. That's just an engine that's been running at high revs… and hasn't come down yet. So we're not going to do anything clever right now. No techniques. Nothing to get right. We're just going to bring the revs down. Step away from the screen if you haven't yet. A few steps is enough. Turn your back to it if you can. [lightly] It'll still be there.

---

## 2. Eyes

[calmly] Start with your eyes, because they've been doing the most work. For hours they've been locked on something about half a metre away. Small text, bright light, fast changes. Now let them go somewhere far. A window… the end of the room… the furthest wall. Just let them rest on something far away. You don't have to look at anything in particular. Let them go soft, a bit lazy, like they're not really focusing on anything. Notice the muscles around your eyes. They might feel tired… or tight… or heavy. Let your eyelids drop halfway. Let your forehead go smooth. Let the space between your eyebrows get wider. [softly] There's nothing to read right now. Nothing to check.

---

## 3. Breath

[slowly] Now one long breath. In through your nose, not too big…

[pause 3s]

[softly] and out through your mouth, slow, like you're letting air out of a tyre. Longer than the in-breath.

[pause 5s]

Again. In through the nose…

[pause 3s]

and the long, slow breath out. Let your shoulders drop with it.

[pause 5s]

[warmly] That long out-breath is the brake pedal. Every time you breathe out longer than you breathe in, your body gets the message that it can come down. You don't have to believe it. Your body knows how this works.

---

## 4. Body

[calmly] Now let's go through your body, quickly, the way you'd walk through a flat switching off the lights. Your jaw. Let your teeth come apart. Let your tongue drop from the roof of your mouth. Your neck. You've probably been leaning forward toward the screen for hours, so let your head float back a little… until it sits right over your shoulders. Your shoulders. Let them drop again, even further than before. Your hands. They've been on the keyboard and the mouse all this time. [lightly] Shake them out. Really shake them, loose, like you're flicking water off your fingers. [softly] And then let them hang. Your feet. Feel the floor under them. Press your toes down for a second… and let go. [warmly] That's where you are. Not in the screen. Not in the project. Here, on this floor, in this room.

---

## 5. Sound

[softly] Now listen. Not to the voice in your head… to the room. What's the furthest sound you can hear? Maybe traffic outside. Birds. Someone talking somewhere. A fan, a fridge, the hum of the building. Just pick the furthest sound and follow it for a moment.

[pause 5s]

[quietly] And now the closest sound. Your own breath, probably. Maybe the rustle of your clothes when you move. [warmly] Your head has been inside a screen for hours, where everything was close and fast and urgent. Out here, the world is going at its own speed. Slower. It's been going at that speed the whole time.

---

## 6. Closing the tabs

[calmly] Now your head might still be holding all three projects at once. Like a browser with too many tabs open. Things you were in the middle of. Things you need to remember. So let's close them, one by one. Not by finishing them. Just by telling them they can wait. Say it to yourself, quietly, in your own words. [softly] The first project. It's saved. It'll be there when I get back. Done.

[pause 4s]

The second one. Saved. It's waiting for me. It doesn't need me right now.

[pause 4s]

The third one. Saved. Nothing breaks while I'm away.

[pause 4s]

[gently] And whatever else is still open. The message you haven't answered. The thing you're worried you'll forget. If it's important, it'll come back. You don't have to hold it. [warmly] Let your head have one empty tab.

---

## 7. Your energy

[warmly] There's a line on your board that fits this moment exactly. It's not about doing more. It's about the opposite. Say it after me. [slowly] I protect my energy.

[pause 6s]

[warmly] That's what this break is. Not lost time. Not you being lazy. It's the thing someone with calm power does on purpose… before the tank is empty, not after. And one more. Short. [slowly] I am breath-led.

[pause 6s]

[softly] Every time today gets loud again, the long breath out is right there. You don't need a track for it. [warmly] You've just done it.

---

## 8. Back

[slowly] Take one more long breath in…

[pause 3s]

and the long, slow breath out.

[pause 5s]

[warmly] Now take your break. A real one. Water. A few steps outside, if you can. Something to eat. Not the phone, not yet. Give your eyes a bit longer on things that are far away. [softly] The work will still be there. Whenever you go back to it, you can bring this slower breath with you. That's enough. [warmly] Go on.
