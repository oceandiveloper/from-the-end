# Padel Track: "After a bad game" (public version, calm talk)

Status: **v1, draft, not voiced yet.** For anyone who's just stressed after a game. No Neville, no LoA terms, no meditation instructions. Kevin's own version with Neville language: `padel-after-v3.md`.
Goal (Kevin, 2026-11-10): a voice that picks people up and calms them down just by talking, and walks them from "terrible game, I failed" to "it's okay, I'll try again next time. Today is over, tomorrow starts fresh."
Use: right after the game, in the car, on the way home. Works as a YouTube video. About 6 minutes. No pauses, ends awake.
Voice: ElevenLabs "James". One paragraph = one voice segment.

Rule check:
- No identity lines (those are Kevin's own), no tactics, no analysis of the game.
- No promise that the next game goes better. Only: you get to try again.
- No claimed feelings: the voice describes what the head does, it doesn't tell you how you feel.
- Always "you".
- Silence: none apart from the gaps between segments.

---

## 1. Picking you up

So. That wasn't your day. You came off the court and something in you is still tight. Maybe it was one point. Maybe it was the whole game. The easy ball you put in the net. The serve that wouldn't come. The moment you knew it was slipping and couldn't turn it around. And now it's playing in your head on repeat, the worst moments, in slow motion, over and over.

If that's where you are right now, you're in the right place. You don't have to do anything. You don't have to sit a certain way or think a certain thing. Keep driving, keep walking, keep packing your bag. Just let me talk for a few minutes. That's all.

## 2. Everyone has these games

First thing. This happens to everyone who plays. Every single person who has ever picked up a racket has driven home after a game like this, going over every mistake. Beginners do it. Club players do it. The best players in the world do it, and they lose matches in front of thousands of people. It's not a sign that something's wrong with you. It's a sign that you care about the game. If you didn't care, it wouldn't sting.

And the replay in your head, that's normal too. Your brain is trying to help. It thinks if it plays the mistake often enough, it can fix it. But it can't fix it now. The game is over. So all the replay does is keep you in a game that already ended.

## 3. What your head is saying

Listen to how your head is talking about this game. It's probably not saying, I missed a few volleys today. It's saying something much bigger. I'm useless. I always do this. I can't handle pressure. Everyone saw it. My partner is annoyed with me. I'll never get better.

That's a lot to get out of one game of padel. One afternoon. A few balls that went the wrong way. Your head took that and turned it into a verdict about who you are. But that's not what happened. What happened is you had a bad game. That's all. A bad game is not a bad player. And it's definitely not a bad person.

## 4. What actually happened

So let's look at what actually happened today. You showed up. You got out there, on a day when maybe you didn't even feel great. You played. You ran for balls. You went for shots, some came off and some didn't. You stood there between points and tried again. Every single point, you tried again. That's what you did today. That's the real story of this game.

And there was more in it than the misses. Think about it for a second. There was at least one shot that landed exactly where you wanted. A rally where you were completely in it. A moment where you laughed, or your partner laughed, or the ball came off the glass in a way that made you both shake your heads. Your head just didn't save those. It only saved the bad bits. But they were there. They're just as much a part of today as the volley into the net.

## 5. One game

Here's the thing about one game. It's one game. If you play for years, you'll play hundreds of them. Some great, some terrible, most somewhere in between. Nobody remembers the score of a game from three weeks ago. You probably don't either. This one will be the same. In a few weeks it'll just be one of many, and you won't even remember which day it was.

What builds a player isn't one good game or one bad game. It's coming back. Again and again. Showing up, playing, losing some, winning some, and showing up again. That's the whole thing. And you're already doing it.

## 6. Today is done

So let's let this one go. Not because it doesn't matter, but because it's finished. The game is over. The score is written down somewhere and it's not going to change. You don't need to carry it home. You don't need to bring it to dinner, or into bed with you tonight. It can stay on the court where it happened.

Today is done. It's in the past now, and the past doesn't need anything else from you.

## 7. Next time

And next time, whenever that is, tomorrow, next week, you walk onto that court fresh. The next game doesn't know anything about today. It starts at zero, for everyone. You get to try again. Not to make up for anything. Not to prove anything. Just to play.

That's all it ever was. A game. And you get to play it again.

So for now, let it go. It's okay. It really is. Go on with your day.
