# Padel Track v2: "After the match" (when you want to tear yourself down)

Status: **v2, pilot for the new flow** (continuous talk, pauses only where you do something). Replaces v1 in the bot. If the flow works for Kevin, all other tracks get rewritten the same way.
Use: after a match that felt bad, before the inner critic takes over the evening. In the car, on the bench, at home. About 8 minutes. Ends awake.
Voice: ElevenLabs "James". `[pause Xs]` = silence added during stitching. One paragraph = one voice segment.
Source for the identity lines: `docs/kevin-2.0.md` (Whole, Playful, Standard "Chooses himself + peace", Level 1 "controlled by fear").

Rule check:
- Identity lines: three, each spoken once.
- No promise ("next time you'll win"), no tactics, no analysis of the game.
- No claimed feelings: the self-criticism is named as weather, never argued with, never denied. Inside the revised scene the voice describes what you do, not what you must feel.
- The revision is lived from inside ("step into it").
- Silence: about 90 s of about 8 min.

---

## 1. Catch

The match is over. And your head is probably already replaying it. The easy ball you should have put away. The volley that went into the net. The serve that didn't come. Maybe a look from your partner, or one you think you saw. It's all running at once, round and round, and it wants you to keep running with it.

You don't have to stop it right now. Just let my voice be the thing you follow for the next few minutes. Wherever you are, in the car, on a bench, at home, you can stay right there. Let your shoulders come down a little. Open your hands if they're tight. Your body just played hard. It worked for you the whole time, every sprint, every stretch for a ball off the glass. It can rest now.

Take a breath in through your nose, slow and deep… and let it go out through your mouth, longer than it came in.

[pause 6s]

One more. In… and a long breath out.

[pause 6s]

---

## 2. The voice

Now notice that voice. The one going through every mistake like a coach who's never satisfied. It sounds very sure of itself. It says things like: you always do this. You can't handle pressure. Everyone saw it. And it says them as if they were facts.

But listen to how it talks. It isn't describing the game. It's handing out a verdict. It takes an hour of hitting a ball over a net and turns it into a judgement about who you are. That's not analysis. That's the old level talking. The one that's controlled by fear, the one that needs every game to prove something.

You don't have to argue with it. Arguing only keeps it going. And you don't have to believe it either. Treat it like weather after a game. Loud, gusty, and passing through. You can hear it without standing in it. You're the one hearing the voice, not the one it's talking about. And weather never stays. It's already changing while you listen.

---

## 3. One point

Now let's do something with one point. The one that stings the most. Don't search for it. It'll come by itself.

[pause 5s]

See it like a short clip on a screen. Where you were standing. Where the ball came from. What happened. Just watch it once from the outside, without commenting on it.

[pause 8s]

Now we play it again. Same court, same ball, same moment. But this time you're not watching. Step into it, as the one you already are. The ball is coming. You see it early. Your feet move. You hit it, and it goes wide. And you breathe out. Maybe you even smile, because it was a good idea that just didn't land. You turn around, tap your partner's racket, and walk back to your spot. By the time you get there, you're already waiting for the next ball. Nothing to prove. Nothing to make up for. Just the next ball, and you're ready for it.

Let that version play one more time, at your own pace. Loose. Light. Already moving on.

[pause 15s]

That's how that point went. That's the one you keep.

---

## 4. Three lines

Now three lines. Let each one land, and say it quietly to yourself after me.

I am whole, whatever the score.

[pause 8s]

I am playful. It's a game, and I get to play it.

[pause 8s]

I choose myself and my peace.

[pause 8s]

---

## 5. Keep one good thing

That voice only told you about the misses. But there was more in that game. Let one good moment come back. A shot that landed exactly where you wanted it. A long rally where you were completely in it. A laugh with your partner between points. Something small is enough.

[pause 6s]

There it is. Now let it be bigger than it was. The sound of the ball. The grip in your hand. The moment you knew it was a good one. Stay in it a little longer than it lasted on the court.

[pause 12s]

That is also today. And that's the part you take with you.

---

## 6. Return

You played. You showed up, you moved, you went for your shots. Whatever the score says, that part is done, and it was yours.

Take one more slow breath in… and a long breath out.

[pause 5s]

Feel your feet on the ground. Notice where you are, the sounds around you, the light. The game is done. You can let it be done now. Go on with your day.
